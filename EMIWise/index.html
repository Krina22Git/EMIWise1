            Understand Your Loan

            The core ideas behind every number on this page, explained simply.

            What is EMI?

            Equated Monthly Instalment — a fixed monthly payment that repays a loan over its tenure, covering both interest and principal.

            Principal vs. Interest

            Principal is the amount you borrowed. Interest is the cost of borrowing it. Every EMI repays a bit of both.

            Reducing-balance interest

            Interest is charged only on the outstanding balance, so as you repay principal, the interest portion of each EMI shrinks.

            Amortization

            The process of paying off a loan gradually through scheduled instalments, detailed month-by-month in an amortization table.

            Loan tenure

            The total time given to repay a loan. Longer tenure means lower EMI but more total interest paid.

            Prepayment

            Paying more than the required EMI. Extra amounts reduce principal directly, shortening the loan and cutting interest.

            Interest savings

            The difference in total interest paid between two repayment strategies — e.g. with and without prepayment.

            Debt-to-income ratio

            Total monthly loan payments divided by monthly income. Lenders and borrowers both use it to gauge affordability.

            Finance Behind EMIWise

            This project applies core finance concepts you can present directly:

            • Time Value of Money — the EMI formula discounts future payments to today's borrowed amount.
            • Loan Amortization — the schedule shows how a lump-sum liability is retired over time.
            • Reducing Balance Method — interest is recalculated each month on the shrinking balance, not the original principal.
            • Interest Cost — total interest reveals the real cost of a loan beyond its headline rate.
            • Debt Burden — the affordability ratio models basic personal-finance risk assessment.
            • Financial Planning — comparing tenures, rates and prepayment shows trade-offs in borrowing decisions.
            • Prepayment Benefits — demonstrates how extra principal payments compound into large interest savings.
            • Loan Comparison — shows why EMI alone is a poor basis for choosing a loan.
